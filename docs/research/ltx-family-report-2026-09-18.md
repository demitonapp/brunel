### Answers
* **Both `LTX-2` and `LTX-2.5` exist and both are open-weights (HIGH).** `LTX-2` created **2026-01-03**; `LTX-2.5` created **2026-07-23**. There is also an intermediate **`LTX-2.3` created 2026-03-04** — the family is 2 → 2.3 → 2.5, not 2 → 2.5.
* **There is no "LTX-2.5" that is API-only.** The weights are published as a split, Comfy-aligned pack (`diffusion_models/…-transformer-bf16.safetensors`, `vae/…`, `text_encoders/gemma4-12b-with-proj-ltx-2.5-bf16.safetensors`, `latent_upscale_models/…`). What *is* gated is the download: `gated: auto` with `extra_gated_button_content: "Agree and Access"` and an `extra_gated_description` that requires acknowledging the privacy policy **and consenting to receive offers and updates including targeted and personalized advertisements** (HIGH, verbatim from the model-card YAML I fetched via `https://huggingface.co/Lightricks/LTX-2.5/resolve/main/README.md`).
* **API-only / separate packaging repos:** `LTX-2.5-Diffusers` (2026-07-26) and `LTX-2.5-Pre-Trained` (2026-08-10) are both **restricted** (login required). The LTX *hosted API* models (`ltx-2-5-fast`, `ltx-2-5-pro`, `ltx-2-3-fast`, `ltx-2-3-pro`) are a distinct, API-only surface (docs.ltx.io).
* **Announce dates:** LTX-2 was **announced 2025-10-23** (GitHub `Lightricks/LTX-Video` README "News"), weights released **2026-01-03**. Paper: arXiv:2601.03233, v1 submitted 2026-01-06.
* **Current recommended model as of 2026-09-18 = LTX-2.5** ("LTX-2.5 is the recommended model", `Lightricks/LTX-2` README, HIGH). `LTX-2` and `LTX-2.3` are legacy but still hosted and non-gated.

### Camera / trajectory control — exact status
* **API-only, fixed enum (HIGH):** `camera_motion` accepts `dolly_in`, `dolly_out`, `dolly_left`, `dolly_right`, `jib_up`, `jib_down`, `static`, `focus_shift`. It is **not a trajectory or intrinsics input** — one of eight presets. Source: https://docs.ltx.io/api-documentation/api-reference/async-video-generation/submit-text-to-video.md
* **Open weights:** only the seven 19B "Camera-Control" LoRAs above — motion *bias*, no numeric path.
* **AVControl paper** (arXiv:2603.24793, Lightricks) documents a **camera-trajectory modality**: "The desired trajectory is encoded as a warped grid on the reference canvas"; from an existing video, "we estimate its full camera parameters and re-render the scene at a new trajectory" (point cloud). The paper says "We publicly release our code and trained LoRA checkpoints" — but I found **no camera-trajectory checkpoint** in the `Lightricks` HF org as of 2026-09-18 → **UNVERIFIED / not released**.

### VRAM reality — exact figures
* **Official minimum for LTX-2.5: "NVIDIA GPU with a minimum 32GB+ VRAM - more is better"**; recommended A100 80GB / H100, 32GB system RAM, 100GB free disk, CUDA 12.7+, Python 3.12+. HIGH — https://docs.ltx.io/open-source-model/getting-started/system-requirements.md
* ComfyUI-LTXVideo README prerequisites: "**CUDA-compatible GPU with 32GB+ VRAM**", "100GB+ free disk space". Its low-VRAM loaders "ensure the correct order of execution and perform the model offloading such that **generation fits in 32 GB VRAM**". HIGH.
* **Community quantization reality (MEDIUM, community-authored, sizes "measured, not estimated"):** LTX-2.5 distilled DiT GGUF — Q2_K 7.9 GB → "yes" on 12 GB; Q3_K_M 10.6 GB → "tight" on 12 GB; `comfy-w4a4` 11.2 GB → "tight" on 12 GB; Q4_K_M 14.2 GB → needs 16 GB+. The **text encoder is a second wall**: Gemma 4 12B is 26.3 GB bf16 / 15.4 GB int8 / 10.6 GB w4a8, and **no GGUF of the Gemma 4 encoder exists**. Source: https://huggingface.co/joeygambino/LTX-2.5-Quantized
* Same source class, blunt quality caveat: "For image and video generation, the **Q2 and Q3 model variants produce poor quality results and are generally unusable for final renders**. They should only be used for basic testing and prototyping." MEDIUM (community) — https://huggingface.co/ruygar/LTX-2.5-Comfy-GGUF
* **RTX 3080 = Ampere `sm_86`.** Native FP8 is Ada `sm_89`+; NVFP4 needs Blackwell `SM ≥ 10`. The LTX-2 source comments it explicitly: *"Triton's fp8e4nv … is only a legal kernel dtype on sm_89+ (Ada). Below that, tl.load of an e4m3fn tensor fails at compile … PyTorch eager conversion still works — that is the software path used when Triton is missing."* So `--quantization fp8-cast` degrades to deterministic nearest rounding on a 3080 (works, but no stochastic-rounding kernel), and `nvfp4-*` is unavailable. HIGH — https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Lightricks/LTX-2/main/packages/ltx-core/src/ltx_core/quantization/fp8_cast.py
* 10–12 GB verdict: **no LTX-2 / 2.3 / 2.5 configuration is officially supported**, and the only community recipes that fit 12 GB are the ones the community itself calls unusable for final renders. The **0.9.x 2B/13B line is the only LTX family with a credible 8–12 GB path** (community Q8: 720×480×121 in under a minute on an 8 GB RTX 4060). INFERENCE, grounded in the figures above.

## 2. Per-model notes — hard-surface / mechanical behaviour and VRAM reality

* **Good news for rigid geometry — Canny is officially the recommended control for exactly this subject class.** The Union Control guide states Canny "**Works well for:** architecture, product shots, geometric compositions, **mechanical objects**, cityscapes — anything with strong, well-defined edges." This is the single most directly relevant official statement for an engineering-history pipeline. I also found the reverse: Blender is named as a preferred depth source ("For synthetic depth renders"), so a deterministic Blender depth pass is a first-class, doc-sanctioned input. (HIGH)
* **The documented failure mode for rigid subjects is temporal deform ("warble").** Lightricks' own tutorial says: *"Objects may subtly deform, wobble, or drift even when they should remain rigid"*, caused by "Motion isn't sufficiently constrained across frames"; it also lists "swirling or 'crawling' surface details … when they should be static", "repeating textures without variation", and "overseen/uniform, synthetic-looking surfaces". Their prescribed fix is precisely the IC-LoRA path: *"Use IC-LoRA (Canny, Depth, or Pose) to anchor motion and structure"*, and specifically **Canny** to "stabilize edges and structural boundaries". (HIGH) — https://ltx.io/blog/how-to-reduce-warble-and-ai-pattern-artifacts-in-ltx-2
  * Note: that page carries a byline date of "February 2, 2026" while discussing LTX-2.5 (released 2026-07-23). Internal date inconsistency on the vendor's own blog → **UNVERIFIED as-dated**, content otherwise consistent with the docs.
* **Prompt/control conflict is a named artefact source.** With IC-LoRA active, motion descriptions in the prompt ("camera pans left") cause "Synthetic / Plastic Surfaces" and loss of texture detail. For a dimensionally-accurate pipeline this matters: keep the prompt to materials/lighting, let the Blender-rendered signal carry all motion. (HIGH)
* **AVControl (Lightricks' own control paper) documents the honest failure modes.** *"Complex character motion"* — "When the reference depth or pose signal contains rapid, intricate character movements, the generated video may exhibit temporal jitter or implausible limb configurations." *"Camera control with fast scene dynamics"* — "per-frame reprojection can produce stretching or ghosting artifacts in the reference canvas, and the LoRA may faithfully reproduce these artifacts rather than correcting them." *"Reference image conditioning"* — "The most notable capability gap relative to VACE is reference image conditioning." It also inherits base-model weaknesses: "character motion, high-frequency details, and audio quality". (HIGH) — https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2603.24793
* **There is direct, official evidence that IC-LoRA conditioning degrades on complex reference signals** — i.e. a dense mechanism animation (many moving rigid parts) is the worst case for the depth/pose path. Canny + a single static camera is the configuration the docs steer you toward. INFERENCE.
* **Text/diagrams/section views: no official accuracy claim found.** The only related vendor statement is the LTX-2.5 decoder improvement: "sharper faces, textures, and **on-screen text**" (HIGH quote from the model card). No benchmark, no failure-mode documentation, no annotation guarantee → **UNVERIFIED**, and I would not treat LTX as a diagram renderer.
* **VRAM: a 3080 (10–12 GB) cannot run LTX-2.x at production quality.** Official floor is 32 GB; even a 24 GB 4090 is described as sitting "below the 32 GB native floor" and needing FP8 + offload + a quantized encoder, and the Gemma 3/4 12B text encoder alone OOM'd at "Peak Usage: 29068 MiB" for community reporters. 12 GB is reachable only via Q2/Q3 GGUF or 4-bit convrot, which the community guidance explicitly calls "unusable for final renders". (HIGH for the official floor; MEDIUM for the community 12 GB numbers.)
* **Ampere penalty.** The 3080 is `sm_86`: native FP8 is Ada `sm_89`+, NVFP4 needs Blackwell. `fp8-cast` falls back to PyTorch eager upcast with deterministic (not stochastic) rounding, and `nvfp4-cast`/`nvfp4-prequant` are unavailable. So the 3080 gets the slowest quantised paths — relevant if you were counting on FP8 to close the gap. (HIGH, from LTX-2 source.)
* **Only the 0.9.x line is realistic on a 3080 today** — but 0.9.x is a synchronous-audio-free, 1216×704-class, 2B/13B model with a **narrower and older** control set, and v0.9.0/0.9.1 are research-only licences. If the 3080 is a hard constraint, 0.9.8 13B distilled + depth/canny IC-LoRA is the only LTX path that fits; if final-render quality is a hard constraint, LTX-2.x needs a 32 GB-class GPU or a rented one. INFERENCE.
* **Format gotcha for the target delivery (9:16 1080×1920 @30fps).** Width must be **divisible by 32**, so 1080 is invalid → generate at **1088×1920** and crop, or use an upscaler. DFR additionally needs ÷64 (1088 and 1920 both satisfy this; 1088/64=17, 1920/64=30). Frame count must be **8n+1**, so 30 fps does **not** land on whole seconds: 6 s = 180 → 177 or 185 frames; 12 s = 360 → 361 frames (12.033 s). The **hosted API offers 24/25/48/50 fps only — no 30 fps** — so a 30 fps deliverable means local generation with a custom `frame_rate` (or generate at 24/25 and retime). Whether local `frame_rate=30` is fully supported end-to-end is **UNVERIFIED**; the docs I read show `frame_rate` as a free parameter and note the transformer "snaps conditioning fps to 60 whenever playback fps is above 30". HIGH for the divisibility rules and the API fps list; INFERENCE for the retiming workaround.
