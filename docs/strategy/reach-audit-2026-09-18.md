# Reach Audit — Brunel vs Animagraffs / Jared Owen

> **Question asked:** how do we get Brunel, as Demiton's marketing arm, to the reach of
> Animagraffs and Jared Owen?
>
> **Short answer:** those two goals are not compatible as currently written, the benchmark
> channels are the wrong benchmarks for a Demiton marketing arm, and the one strategy that
> serves both is already in `docs/strategy/spec.md` §3 — ranked last.

Measured 2026-09-18 from the live channel pages. All view counts as displayed by YouTube.
Supersedes nothing; it is evidence for a decision that `docs/strategy/spec.md` §1 currently makes
in the opposite direction.

---

## 1. The contradiction, stated once

| | Reach like Animagraffs / Jared Owen | Marketing arm of Demiton |
|---|---|---|
| Audience | global consumer, 2–5M subs | AU civil construction, a few thousand firms |
| Subject test | "does a stranger already know this object?" | "does this buyer have this problem?" |
| Geography | none — zero of Animagraffs' 48 subjects require a country | Australia only |
| Success metric | views | named accounts in pipeline |
| Value of a marginal view | high | ~zero |

`docs/strategy/spec.md` §1 locks **"Australia only, for at least 12 months. Every subject Australian."**
That decision is defensible for Demiton and it directly caps the reach goal. Both cannot be
optimised. The measured cost of that clause is in §3 below.

If Brunel hits 2M subscribers as specified, roughly 99.9% of that reach is outside Demiton's
addressable market. If Brunel serves Demiton's market precisely, it will never approach these
channels' numbers, because the market is smaller than one of their videos' view counts.

**This audit does not resolve that for you. It prices both sides and recommends the overlap.**

## 2. What the benchmarks actually are (measured, not estimated)

| | Animagraffs | Jared Owen |
|---|---|---|
| Subscribers | **1.93M** | **4.43M** |
| Videos | **48** | **121** |
| First upload | ~7 years ago | ~15 years ago (11 years of consistent output) |
| Output rate | **~7/year** — one every 7–8 weeks | **~11/year** |
| Length, now | **46 min – 1h 24m** | 10–20 min |
| Length, at launch | 1:38 | 0:52 – 4:00 |
| Median views (30-video sample) | **4.3M** | **5.5M** |
| Shorts on channel | **4** | **4** |

### Four things this data says that the plan currently assumes otherwise

**a. This is a decade, not a 12-month slate.** Animagraffs took ~5 years to 1M subs and 7 to
1.93M. Jared Owen has been uploading since 2011. `docs/strategy/spec.md` §3 is a 12-month slate aimed at a
position both channels took 7–11 years and 48–121 videos to reach.

**b. Both started small and scaled scope with the audience.** Jared Owen's early catalogue is
clicky pens, Rubik's cubes, and a 1:44 "How big is the Solar System" that has **28M views**.
Animagraffs' first upload was a 1:38 sewing machine. The plan opens on a 10–18 minute Snowy
Mountains Scheme flagship requiring three capabilities that do not exist (C1, C2, C3) — the
hardest possible first attempt, at the point of least knowledge about what the audience wants.

**c. Shorts are not their growth engine.** Four Shorts each, all cut from existing long-form
renders. `docs/strategy/spec.md` §4 calls the Shorts funnel "the growth engine" and specifies 1–2/week —
52–104 Shorts/year against the benchmarks' 4 lifetime. Neither benchmark supports that claim.
(§4 below shows Shorts *are* a large lever in the construction-machinery niche specifically —
but that is a different argument from the one in the spec, and it points at different subjects.)

**d. Both are past their own peak.** Animagraffs' three most recent: 1.1M, 667k, 1.1M against a
4.3M median. Jared Owen's three most recent: 408k, 1.0M, 1.7M against a 5.5M median. "Their level
of reach" is a level they are no longer hitting per-video.

## 3. The Australia clause has a measured price

Jared Owen, *"What's inside the Sydney Opera House?"* — **1.7M views**, against his 5.5M median
for that era. Same creator, same format, same production quality, same title pattern. The
Australian subject returned **31% of his median**.

That is the cleanest available estimate of the geographic penalty, and it is on a landmark far more
globally recognised than the Snowy Scheme, the Overland Telegraph, or the Trans-Australian Railway.

It also means `docs/strategy/spec.md` §3 Episode 6 (Opera House) is **already made, by a 4.4M-subscriber
channel, three years ago.** Episode 4 (Harbour Bridge) is the only other globally recognised
subject on the slate.

Of the 12 planned episodes, on a reach test: 2 are globally recognised (one taken), 3 are
mechanism-evergreens with global demand (§4), and **7 have effectively no global search demand.**

**"how a tunnel boring machine works"**

| Sabin Civil Engineering, "Channel Tunnel" | 5.6M |
| **Shorts on the same topic** | **7.1M, 4.3M, 2.4M, 2.3M, 1.6M, 1.5M, 1.3M** |

**"how a tower crane works"**

| Result | Views |
|---|---|
| Art of Engineering, "How Tower Cranes Build Themselves" | **7.5M** |
| Next best long-form | 55k |
| Shorts | 2.4M, 133k |

**The finding:** demand for premium mechanism animation of construction plant is in the millions,
and the supply at Animagraffs grade is **absent**. The best dedicated TBM animation on the
platform has 75k views. This is the clearest unoccupied niche found in this audit, and it is the
only subject class that is simultaneously:

- an Animagraffs-shaped title ("How a tunnel boring machine works"),
- globally searched, no geography required,
- and **Demiton's actual domain** — the machines its customers own and operate.

`docs/strategy/spec.md` §3 contains exactly these episodes — **5 (TBM), 9 (metro tunnelling), 11 (hydro
station)** — labels them "the search workhorses", and schedules them at **months 4–12**, behind
seven Australian-history episodes with no global demand.

**That ordering is the single largest correctable error in the plan.**

The Shorts nuance: in *this* niche, unlike the benchmark channels', Shorts do millions of views on
exactly the mechanism moments the harness renders. The Shorts funnel is a real lever here — for
machinery subjects, not for Australian history.

## 5. The benchmarks are the wrong benchmarks

Four channels in adjacent territory, measured today:

| Channel | Subs | Videos | Cadence | Production |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Practical Engineering** | **4.83M** | 250 | fortnightly | a host, a backyard model, light motion graphics |
| **Jared Owen** | 4.43M | 121 | ~11/yr | mid-weight 3D |
| **The B1M** | **4.07M** | **923** | **weekly** | journalism, stock footage, motion graphics |
| **Animagraffs** | **1.93M** | 48 | ~7/yr | **the highest render quality of the four** |

**Render quality is inversely correlated with reach across this set.** The channel with
Animagraffs-grade 3D has the fewest subscribers of the four. The two largest use no bespoke 3D at
all. Reach in this space tracks **cadence, topic selection and a human presence** — not L4 renders.

`ROADMAP.md` §4 prices L3 at 160 h/episode and L4 at 380 h/episode. On this evidence those hours
do not buy reach. They buy the Animagraffs position: the most admired, third of the subscribers.

**The B1M is the benchmark this question should have named.** 4.07M subs, weekly, construction
industry, commercially sponsored by construction firms and vendors — i.e. it *is* a marketing
vehicle for the construction sector, which is the stated job. And it is already in the Australian
territory: *"Australia Desperately Needs This Rail Line"* — 868k views, 3 days old;
*"The $135BN Bullet Train That Could Save Australia"* — 1.1M, 1 month old.

The B1M will cover Snowy 2.0 before Brunel ships Episode 1, with a two-person crew and archive
footage, and get ~1M views for it.

## 6. The capacity arithmetic does not close

From `render-bench.json` and `README.md`: 1.97 s/frame at 384×682/8spp, extrapolating to **~62
s/frame** at 1080×1920/32spp on the M1 — **~37 hours for 72 seconds of video.**

| Target | Frames | M1 render | 3080 node at 10× |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12-minute episode | 21,600 | **372 h** (15.5 days continuous) | ~37 h |
| The spec's 26 videos/year | 561,600 | 9,672 h | ~967 h |

Add `ROADMAP.md` §4 human hours: at L2 (70 h/ep) the spec's 26-video cadence is **1,820 founder
hours/year** before render, scripting, research or the Demiton day job. At L3 it is 4,160.

Animagraffs produces **7** videos/year at 46–84 minutes as full-time work with seven years of
accumulated asset library. The spec proposes **26/year** — 3.7× the output — from one founder with
an empty `library/`.

**Cadence and weight cannot both be matched.** On the benchmark evidence, reach comes from weight
and topic, and The B1M shows cadence works only with a production model that does not render
anything from scratch.

Zero videos published. `eval/audience.md` does not exist. `goldens/` is empty. Discussing CTR
against a 1.93M-subscriber channel is several orders of magnitude removed from the binding
constraint, which is: **one finished video has not yet left the building.**

## 8. Recommendation

### Decide this first, in one line, and write it into `docs/strategy/spec.md` §1

> **Is Brunel a reach asset that Demiton sponsors, or a demand-gen asset for Demiton?**

The current spec says the second and measures the first. That is why the slate, the cadence and
the metrics disagree with each other.

### Option A — Reach first, Demiton as sponsor (recommended)

Drop "every subject Australian." Keep the mechanism-first, sourced wedge. Demiton is a 15-second
bumper, a link and a case-study page — the B1M model, which is a proven construction-marketing
vehicle at 4M subs.

**Concretely, against the current plan:**

1. **Invert the slate.** Ship the mechanism evergreens first: TBM, tower crane, hydro station,
   segmental lining, dragline, concrete batching. Every one is an "How a X works" title with
   proven millions in demand and zero premium supply (§4), and every one is Demiton's domain.
   Episodes 5, 9 and 11 become 1, 2 and 3.
2. **Kill the Snowy flagship as the opener.** It needs C1+C2+C3 (none exist), it is the hardest
   first video, and it carries the geographic penalty measured in §3. Keep it as Episode 8, after
   the format is proven and the capabilities exist.
3. **Start short.** 4–8 minutes, one mechanism, matching both benchmarks' actual launch behaviour
   — not 10–18 minutes. Scope grows with the audience, not before it.
4. **Set cadence from capacity, not aspiration.** 12–15 videos/year at 6–10 minutes is achievable
   and is already 2× Animagraffs' rate. 26/year is not (§6).
5. **Shorts against machinery subjects only**, cut from the same render. Proven millions in that
   niche (§4); no evidence for it against Australian history.

**Expected trajectory, on the benchmark evidence:** 100–300k subs at 24 months if the format
works. 2M is a 5–8 year proposition, and both benchmarks are currently declining per-video.

### Option B — Demiton first, abandon the reach goal as stated

The channel becomes a B2B asset: 2–4 minute mechanism explainers of the customer's problem,
distributed on LinkedIn and industry press, measured in named accounts rather than views. The
`local` backend at L1–L2 is already sufficient. This is achievable in weeks, not years, and it is
what "marketing arm" literally means. The harness's real Demiton value is the
`README.md` "engineering simulation later" arm — one animated cross-section of a *customer's*
actual job is worth more to Demiton than a million views of the Harbour Bridge.

### Option C — Both, sequenced

Option B now (it is cheap and it pays the bills), Option A from month 6, once H1–H5 are closed and
one video has actually shipped.

### What does not change under any option

Close the "Now" block in `docs/strategy/spec.md` §14 — H1, H3, H2, H15 — before another strategy document
is written. Per H17, this document is itself the failure mode it describes if it does not turn
into an assertion or a deleted episode from the slate.
