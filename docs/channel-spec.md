# Channel Spec — Australia First

> **The goal is viewers. The harness is only the cheapest way for one founder to serve viewers.**
> This document is the product spec. The harness, the specs, the fact-gate — all of it exists to
> make the videos in §3 compelling enough to grow. When a harness decision and a storytelling
> decision conflict, the storytelling decision wins.

**Decision locked (2026-09-18):** trial in Australia for at least the first 12 months. Every
subject is Australian. International subjects wait until the format and the audience are proven.

---

## 1. The niche

### One-sentence thesis

> **"How Australia was built — the machines and mega-projects that did the impossible, explained
> with nothing guessed and the receipts shown."**

### Why this is winnable for a solo founder

The competitive reality is the *opposite* of the global one:

- **Globally**, Animgraffs and Jared Owen own "how it works." A solo founder cannot out-render them.
- **In Australia, nobody owns "how it was built."** There is documentary TV (broadcast, slow,
  no mechanism clarity) and a handful of small YouTubers (no animation quality, no sourcing
  discipline). There is **no** Animgraffs/Jared Owen of Australian engineering.

So the year-one benchmark is not Animgraffs. It is a near-empty niche. The goal for the first 12
months is simple and achievable: **be the definitive Australian engineering channel.** That is the
beachhead from which "overtake Animgraffs" becomes a year-3 question, not a year-1 one.

### The wedge (what we do that no one else does)

1. **Mechanism-first, sourced.** Every video shows *how it works* (the cutaway, the section, the
   cycle) AND cites the primary record on screen ("ICE Archives, 1828" / "National Archives of
   Australia, 1955"). The fact ledger is public, not internal. **Trust is the moat.**
2. **Story-driven, not just explained.** The competitors answer "how does it work." We answer
   "how was the impossible done, and what did it cost to do it." The mechanism is the *hero*, the
   story is the *engine*.
3. **The tunnelling spine.** Marc Brunel's shield (1825) → the Snowy Scheme's tunnels (1949–74) →
   Snowy 2.0's TBMs (now). One 200-year story about one machine. It makes the obscure subject
   relevant and the mega-project authentic, and it is the natural through-line of the whole year.

### The honest constraints (state them, then design around them)

- **"Australian" caps global reach but buys a loyal local audience and zero competition.** Frame
  every subject universally ("how a nation harnessed a mountain range"), not parochially.
- **Mega-projects are systems of systems.** The discipline that makes Animgraffs unbeatable is
  *one object, one mechanism, deeply*. We hold the same line: **one system per video.**
- **The people problem is real.** Snowy is a story *about* workers, and our `crew` generator
  produces scale witnesses, not people. We resolve this once (§4), before the flagship.

## 2. The storytelling system

This is the most important section. It is a **repeatable engine**, not a list of tips. Every video
runs through the same five beats, and the harness exists to make those beats cheap to render.

### 2.1 The five-beat story engine

```
1. IMPOSSIBLE PROBLEM   Why this had to happen, and why everyone said it couldn't.
2. THE BOLD BET         Someone proposes a way. The mechanism is introduced as the ANSWER.
3. THE BREAKDOWN        The obstacle that nearly killed it. Peak tension.
4. THE BREAKTHROUGH     The mechanism's moment of truth — shown doing the impossible.
5. THE LEGACY           What it changed — and why it matters to you right now.
```

**The mechanism is the protagonist.** It is not a list of parts; it is a character with a goal
(dig under a river, hold back a mountain, turn falling water into the grid) and obstacles. Every
part is a plot point. This is the single biggest unlock: it turns a "how it works" lecture into a
story the viewer *wants to know the ending of*.

Worked example — the Snowy flagship, one paragraph:

> *Australia in 1949 has a river (the Snowy) that flows uselessly into the sea, while the inland
> plains that grow the nation's food are drying out — separated by the Great Dividing Range. The
> bet: carve 145 km of tunnels *through* the range, move the river *backwards over the mountain*,
> and turn the fall into electricity. The breakdown: the rock, the floods, the men who died
> (121 of ~100,000, from 30+ countries). The breakthrough: the moment a tunnel breaks through the
> far side — the same machine Marc Brunel invented in 1825, grown to the size of a building. The
> legacy: every light in the east-coast grid, and the pumped-hydro "battery" being dug under the
> same mountains today.*

### 2.2 The cold open (first 15–30 seconds)

Never open on a title card or a logo. Open on one of three things, in order of preference:

| Open | Pattern | Snowy example |
|---|---|---|
| **The disaster** | The worst moment, mid-crisis, no context | *"In 2022, a 2,000-tonne tunnel boring machine stopped moving under the Snowy Mountains — and it never moved again."* |
| **The scale** | A number the viewer cannot picture, then the picture | *"This machine is 200 metres long, weighs as much as a warship, and it's buried under 700 metres of rock right now."* |
| **The stakes** | "Every time you…, part of that…" | *"Every time you turn on a light in Sydney, part of that power has already been through a mountain — twice."* |

Then pose the problem, *withhold* the answer, and cut to the title. The mechanism is revealed only
after the problem has made it necessary.

### 2.3 Pacing: one "aha" every ~90 seconds

A viewer's attention is a leaky bucket. Plug a leak every ~90 seconds with a reveal — a moment
where they think *"oh, THAT's how/why."* These are the mechanism payoffs:

- *Why a shield and not a pickaxe?* — because the ground is wet and will collapse; the shield is a
  travelling wall.
- *Why does the arch hold?* — because the load is pushed *down the curve* into the ground, not up
  into the air.
- *Why are the Opera House sails all the same curve?* — because they're segments of *one sphere*,
  the trick that made an unbuildable shape buildable.

**The withhold is the tool.** State the problem fully before revealing the solution, so the solution
lands as a payoff. If the reveal comes before the problem, it's a lecture.

### 2.4 Scale anchoring — the mandatory "whoa" moment

Every hero shot needs a scale reference the viewer already knows. Without it, "11.43 m" is a number,
not a thing. With it, "a shield as wide as a house, taller than a double-decker bus, and one man in
each of 36 cells" is a picture. The harness already has `crew` (1.70 m) for this — use it as a
*dedicated scale bar*, not a character (see §4).

### 2.5 The "so what" closer

Every video ends on why it matters **now**, in the viewer's life. This is the difference between
"interesting" and "worth subscribing to":

- Snowy → the grid you use today, and the battery being dug under the same mountains.
- Harbour Bridge → the city you live in, and why the arch still holds.
- Snowy 2.0 → the energy transition, and why it's so much harder than the first time.

The closer also plants the next video ("and the machine that dug this is the same one Brunel
invented in 1825 — that's next time").

### 2.6 Fact-as-trust: cite on screen, as a beat

"We don't guess" is a *storytelling device*, not a footnote. At the moment of peak claim, show the
receipt — a two-second on-screen citation ("ICE Archives, 1828" / "National Archives of Australia,
series A1200"). It reads as confidence, and it is the single thing that separates us from the
AI-slop history channels flooding the platform. The fact ledger becomes a linked page per video.

## 4. The production system (one founder, one harness)

### The principle: cadence and story beat polish, not render polish

A 12-minute video with a great story and L1–L2 visuals will beat a 20-minute video with L4 visuals
and no story. Spend the founder's scarce hours on the **hook, the hero frame, and the last line**
(the three things the ROADMAP already says the human owns), and let the harness make the *bad*
versions of a shot cheap so those three things can be iterated 30 times instead of 3.

### Cadence

- **One long-form video every 2–3 weeks** (10–18 min). This is the growth engine.
- **1–2 Shorts/Reels per week, cut from the same render** — the funnel, not a second production.
  The harness re-renders a 9:16 crop of the same spec for free; the founder writes one hook line.

### What to build in the harness, in the order the slate needs it

The content roadmap drives the engineering roadmap — not the reverse.

1. **Mode C (diagram/data) — before Episode 1.** The terrain map, the cross-section, the animated
   dimension callout. This is the single highest-leverage build; the Snowy flagship is *made of*
   Mode C.
2. **Geospatial/terrain input** (DEM heightfield + real-world extents). "1 BU = 1 m" was made for
   this. Needed by Episode 1.
3. **Diagrammatic water flow** (animated arrows/sheets, not fluid sim). Needed by Episodes 1, 3, 11.
4. **"As of" facts in the fact-gate** for the moving Snowy 2.0 numbers. Needed by Episode 3.
5. **The character decision — resolve it once, now.** The peg-doll "scale witness" is the worst of
   both. Choose: **(a) people-free diagrammatic style + a dedicated scale bar + archival
   photographs**, or **(b) real characters.** For year one, choose **(a)** — it is honest, cheaper,
   and Animgraffs proves a people-free style can win. Revisit characters only when a story
   *requires* them (Episode 10, the soldiers).
6. **Dual aspect ratio from the spec level.** Long-form is 16:9; Shorts are 9:16. Both from the
   same spec, so the camera/framing lessons are learned once, not twice.
7. **Thumbnail rendering.** The harness already renders hero frames; render the thumbnail from the
   same scene (subject + one glowing callout + a scale reference). Single-founder leverage.

### The "one system per video" rule, enforced

If an episode can't state its *one* mechanism in a sentence, it is two episodes. The Snowy Scheme is
not one video — it is three (the story, the tunnels, the power station). This rule is what keeps a
mega-project from becoming an overview.

## 5. Gaining viewers: the packaging and retention system

Content quality gets watch-time; **packaging gets the click, and the click is what grows a
channel.** These are versioned, testable artifacts — treat them exactly like code.

### Titles — three proven patterns

1. **"How [X did the impossible]"** — *How Australia moved a river over a mountain.*
2. **"[Number] [unit] of [thing] + [impossible verb]"** — *A 53,000-tonne arch that holds itself up.*
3. **"The [object] everyone said couldn't be built"** — *The geometry everyone said couldn't be built.*

Rules: a concrete number or a concrete object, never both abstract; no colons; under ~60 chars;
the subject visible in the first three words.

### Thumbnails

One subject, one glowing callout, high contrast, a scale reference, and a cutaway where possible.
The harness renders these from the actual scene — no stock art, no Photoshop by hand. This is a
real single-founder advantage: the thumbnail and the hero frame come from the same deterministic
scene, so the promise in the thumbnail is *the same thing* the video delivers.

### Retention mechanics (baked into the script, not the edit)

- **Open loops** — pose a question, answer it 90 seconds later ("but there was a problem nobody had
  foreseen…").
- **Chapter cliffhangers** — each beat ends by naming the next beat's stakes.
- **The one number** — each video has exactly one surprising fact the viewer will repeat ("the
  Snowy moved a river *backwards over a mountain*"). Design the whole video around it.

### The Shorts/Reels funnel

A Short is not a mini-video; it is a **trailer for the long-form**. One mechanism reveal, one
"whoa," one "see the full story." Cut from the same render. It earns the *browse* discovery that
feeds the *search* evergreen.

## 6. The metrics — the ratchet applied to storytelling

The harness already has an eval ledger for *render* quality. It needs a second one for *audience*
quality. Same discipline as `LESSONS.md`: every entry carries a check, or it doesn't belong.

| Metric | What it measures | The lever |
|---|---|---|
| **CTR** (impressions → click) | Thumbnail + title | Version the pack, keep the winner |
| **AVD % / retention at 30s, 2 min, mid-roll** | The cold open and the 90-second aha cadence | Version the hook, keep the winner |
| **Subscribers per 1,000 views** | Trust (the fact-as-trust device) | Cite on screen; ship the ledger page |
| **Search vs. Browse/Suggested split** | Evergreen vs. discovery health | The TBM/mechanism episodes feed search; Shorts feed browse |

**The versioning rule:** the hook, the title, the thumbnail, and the last line are versioned
artifacts. Change one, measure, keep the winner — the exact "ratchet" the ROADMAP promises, aimed
now at the thing that actually grows the channel instead of at the renderer.

`eval/audience.md` records each video's numbers and the *one change* that moved them. Monotonic
improvement in that file is the proof the ratchet is working.
